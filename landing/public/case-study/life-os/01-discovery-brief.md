# Page 1

## Introduction

We started with a builder's question: why do people who own several good tools still rebuild their day by hand? We read three years of Microsoft Work Trend Index data, re-read the HBR app-toggling study, traced three academic sources, and ran one synthetic dry run to test our interview questions. We interviewed no one.

Our objective is to design a daily-planning approach that addresses the reconciliation burden of self-managing knowledge workers in India and improves how calmly they move through a day. The builder's existing product stays out of Phases 1 to 3.

## The global scenario: work is fragmented before the app opens

Work software has never been richer. Chat, meetings, documents, calendars, task apps and AI assistants all sit one click apart.

The people using them describe something else. In Microsoft's 2025 survey, 48% of employees and 52% of leaders said their work feels chaotic and fragmented [E]. The same report found that the top 20% of users by ping volume get a meeting, email or chat ping about every two minutes in an eight-hour day. That is a high-volume cohort, not every worker.

| External fragmentation | Self-management fragmentation |
|---|---|
| Meetings interrupt planned work | A backlog isn't a realistic plan |
| Chat and email create reactive queues | Habits and routines compete with work |
| Calendar and task priority live in different systems | Focus sessions sit apart from the task plan |
| Work arrives through many channels | People re-prioritize after every disruption |
| Notifications reopen settled choices | Keeping the system current becomes work |

**68%** of people say they don't have enough uninterrupted focus time during the workday. Microsoft Work Trend Index, 9 May 2023; 31,000 people in 31 countries [E].

**~1,200** toggles per user per day. HBR (Murty, Dadlani and Das): 20 teams, 137 users, three Fortune 500 companies, up to five weeks. Each switch cost a little over two seconds, adding up to just under four hours a week, about 9% of annual work time [E].

*Pair type: prevalence plus cause.* The HBR sample is enterprise staff outside India, and it measures toggling, not personal planning.

*Visual elements to design:* the two-column cluster above as two stacked cards; two stat cards side by side; a small chain of six chips (chat, task app, calendar, focus timer, routine log, review) showing one task changing hands.

## The Indian perspective: high AI use, high strain

Indian knowledge workers report more AI use and more strain than the global sample at the same time. We don't read that as a failure. High adoption means the appetite for help already exists.

| Belief | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **83%** would delegate as much work as possible to AI to lessen their workloads (Microsoft India, 1 Jun 2023) [E] | **92%** use AI at work, against **75%** globally (Microsoft and LinkedIn, 16 May 2024; 31,000 respondents, 31 countries) [E] |

*Pair type: belief plus behavior.* The pairing suggests openness to AI help is already common. It doesn't show that people will let AI move their own calendar. The surveys sit a year apart and cover different people, and the India release gives no India-only sample size.

*Visual elements to design:* a split card, belief on the left and behavior on the right, with a thin year label under each figure.

## Impact on the self-managing worker

| Compared with the whole | Depth within the group |
|---|---|
| **78%** of Indian workers lack uninterrupted focus, against **68%** globally. Both come from the 2023 Work Trend Index [E] | **76%** say they lack the time and energy to get their work done (Microsoft India, 1 Jun 2023) [E] |

*Pair type: disparity plus severity.*

We narrow to people who set their own priorities, receive work from several channels, and keep a flexible or hybrid calendar. Their life moment is the shift from being handed a plan to owning one.

Our HMW names a role plus a working phase. A tighter life-phase wording is available, such as early-to-mid career people whose calendar became their own. We kept the broader framing by choice (decision D1, end of this file).

**The translation loop (conceptual, not measured):** Capture, Prioritize, Schedule, Execute, Recover, Re-plan. Our hypothesis is that the last two steps cost more than the first [H].

*Visual elements to design:* two bars for 78% and 68% with the gap labeled; a six-step horizontal funnel with the last two steps highlighted.

## Signals, not interviews

Three kinds of evidence stand in for interviews: telemetry and surveys (Microsoft, HBR), public voices (Hacker News commenters), and a synthetic dry run with three invented personas. We recruited no participant.

**Operational signal: switching tax.** Roughly 1,200 toggles a day at a little over two seconds each is a cost nobody sees on a calendar [E].

**Relational signal: other people's calendars.** 30% of meetings now span multiple time zones, and meetings starting after 8 pm are up 16% year on year (Microsoft, 17 Jun 2025; the report's body says "nearly a third") [E].

**Emotional signal: the pseudoproductive retreat.**

> "If my next steps/actions are unclear, it's easier to just take a break or default to doing something pseudoproductive (e.g. organizing my calendar, todo list) rather than take on a meaningful new project."
>
> Hacker News commenter, tool user, [3 Jan 2021](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=25623395) [P]

Meeting organizers and managers set much of the calendar, and we have no verbatim from one yet. Microsoft's India data hints that part of that calendar bends: 46% of Indian workers say their absence from half or more of their meetings would go unnoticed [E, stated view].

Some people solve this with one tool. Another commenter wrote: "After trying various todo lists and kanban boards I finally settled on using my google calendar which works perfectly as both my todo list and scheduler" ([8 Feb 2016](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=11061933)) [P]. Consolidation is optional, and a satisfied minimalist is a real comparison group.

**Synthetic dry run [S], hypotheses only.** Three invented personas answered our interview guide. We use it to fix the guide, never as evidence.

| Question | Pattern in the dry run | Hypothesis to test |
|---|---|---|
| What happens when the day changes at 2 PM? | The plan goes stale and the user patches it by hand | Re-planning is the failure moment |
| Would you let AI rearrange your day? | Yes if it shows what moved, why, and offers undo | Advisory beats silent |
| Where do habits live? | Outside the work calendar, dropped first | Routines lose to visible meetings |
| Do you plan most mornings? | One persona never does and loses tasks for days | Two problems may sit under one HMW |

*Visual elements to design:* three icon-led insight blocks (a switch, a clock with a globe, a chat bubble); one pull-quote card with an oversized quotation mark and italic attribution; a small "public signal versus synthetic" legend.

## Emotional arc: a day that starts intentional and turns reactive

*Caption:* one weekday, first check to last, for a knowledge worker who plans in the morning. *Scope:* one plausible path, not a universal one.

| Stage | Touchpoints | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Morning setup | Reviews inbox, calendar and overdue work; picks two priorities | Hopeful, in control |
| 2. First focus block | Starts the top task; a chat request lands; opens another app to capture it | Focused but fragile |
| 3. Midday squeeze | A meeting overruns; planned tasks no longer fit; the personal routine slips first | Pressured |
| 4. Afternoon re-plan | Drags blocks between list and calendar; stops updating the plan accurately | Overloaded |
| 5. Evening close | Some real work done; open items with no next slot | Relief mixed with unfinished |

**Curve shape:** a descending zigzag with a partial recovery at shutdown. Small wins happen all day. Each unplanned change adds a coordination cost, and the costs pile up.

**Synthetic scene [S].** At 2 PM a meeting runs 45 minutes over and three afternoon blocks no longer fit. The user drags tasks between a list and a calendar for about 15 minutes and still isn't sure the new plan is better. The 15 minutes is invented.

**Research connection.** Leroy (2009) studied attention residue when switching between work tasks [E; title and venue confirmed, abstract not retrieved]. If the residue effect holds for daily planning, cleaner transitions and a clear close would matter as much as faster task entry [H].

*Visual elements to design:* five vertical stage columns; a zigzag line through 12 touchpoints with a face icon on each (hopeful, focused, strained, overloaded, mixed); anchor icons for a sunrise planner, a focus ring, a calendar collision, drag arrows and a closing moon; a one-line scope disclaimer under the chart.

## Impact framework: what a calmer day should improve

| Dimension | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive Load | Effort spent remembering and reconciling | Primary |
| Time Control | Seeing what realistically fits | Primary |
| Focus | Staying with one task long enough | Primary |
| Trust and Agency | Automation that is understandable, reversible, user-controlled | Primary |
| Follow-through | Intentions becoming scheduled, completed actions | Ripple |
| Wellbeing | Less guilt and spillover | Ripple |

We focus on the four primary dimensions. If users spend less effort maintaining the system and keep control of how the day changes, we would expect follow-through and wellbeing to improve as a ripple effect. We don't measure either here.

**Planning research, with limits.** Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006) found a medium-to-large effect of if-then plans on goal attainment across 94 tests (d = .65) [E]. Sheeran, Listrom and Gollwitzer (2024) found effects across 642 tests (.27 to .66), larger when plans had an if-then format [E]. Calendar blocking isn't an if-then plan, and neither effect size predicts a product result.

*Visual elements to design:* a radar chart of the six dimensions with the four primary ones filled and the two ripple ones outlined.

## Opportunities identified: directions, not solutions

| # | Direction | Layer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reduce cross-system reconciliation | Infrastructure (tools) |
| 2 | Reduce recovery effort after a disruption | Practice (personal routine) |

The usual policy, community and infrastructure layers suit a social-care topic. We adapted them to tools, practice and norms for a personal-productivity topic.

## Problem statement and expected impact

> **How might we help self-managing knowledge workers in India turn scattered work and personal commitments into a realistic, adaptable daily plan that remains calm enough to use throughout the day?**

**Why now.** Tools are adding automation and integrations faster than people learn to trust them. India pairs 92% AI use with 78% reporting focus scarcity [E]. The opening is a single, understandable daily decision loop.

| Ripple | If we answer the HMW well |
|---|---|
| Individual | Less effort deciding what to do next |
| Immediate relational | Fewer missed or conflicting commitments with teammates and household |
| Work and personal system | Better follow-through on priority work and on routines |

## Interview guide for the next phase (6 to 10 participants)

1. Walk me through yesterday, from first work check to last.
2. Which apps did you use to capture, plan, schedule, focus and reflect?
3. Show me the last time a task became a calendar slot or a work session.
4. When the day changes, what do you drop first, and why?
5. Which parts of your system do you maintain only because another tool requires it?
6. When did an automation or AI suggestion feel useful? When did it feel wrong?
7. What would you never want an assistant to infer or change on its own?
8. When did you last lose a task for more than a day? What happened?
9. Which commitments exist only in your head, not on any calendar or list?

**Recruit:** people using at least two of task manager, calendar, habit tracker, focus timer and AI assistant. Screen with "Do you plan your day most mornings?" and recruit both yes and no. Include satisfied calendar-only users and both manual and auto-schedule fans. Questions 8, 9 and the screen came from the dry run. They fix the guide and prove nothing.
